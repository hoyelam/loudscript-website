LoudScript / Screenshot OCR guide

Screenshot OCR text-to-speech for Mac

Read words that are trapped inside images, videos, scanned documents, and app interfaces. LoudScript captures a screen region, extracts the visible text with macOS OCR, and starts speaking.

By Ho Ye Lam · Updated August 19, 2026

Turn on-screen text into speech

  1. Press Option-Command-R when no text is selected.
  2. Drag over the words you want LoudScript to capture.
  3. Listen while built-in macOS OCR extracts the text and LoudScript begins playback.

Screen capture requires macOS Screen Recording permission. LoudScript prompts for it when needed and links directly to the relevant System Settings page.

When screenshot reading is useful

Text selection is the fastest route when an app exposes its content normally. Screenshot OCR covers the cases where it does not.

  • Words embedded in screenshots, slides, diagrams, or scanned pages.
  • Text inside video frames or remote-desktop sessions.
  • PDF and ebook viewers that disable or limit selection.
  • Labels, alerts, and custom interfaces that do not expose selectable text.

How screenshot OCR handles your content

LoudScript creates a temporary capture for the screen area you choose. The app deletes that temporary file after OCR finishes or fails. The extracted OCR text is not sent to product analytics.

Speech synthesis stays on your Mac when you use Apple voices or a downloaded Supertonic, Kokoro, or PocketTTS model. Optional AI formatting is separate: if you enable it, the text being formatted can be sent to a third-party provider. See the privacy policy for the complete explanation.

Get clearer OCR results

  • Capture only the text you need instead of a full busy screen.
  • Use a larger zoom level when characters are small.
  • Avoid overlapping windows, cursors, and controls where possible.
  • Review names, numbers, and technical notation before relying on the spoken result.

OCR accuracy depends on image clarity, contrast, language, and layout. LoudScript keeps the recognized text available so you can inspect or replay it from reading history.

Use the fastest reading path

If the words are selectable, skip capture and send them directly to LoudScript through the Services menu or global shortcut.
