How to read selected text aloud on Mac

Highlight words in a browser, editor, email, PDF viewer, or another Mac app. LoudScript reads the selection with a local voice and keeps playback controls above your other windows.

By Ho Ye Lam · Updated August 4, 2026

Read selected text in three steps

  1. Select the text you want to hear in any app that allows text selection.
  2. Start reading by opening the Services menu and choosing Read with LoudScript, or press Option-Command-R.
  3. Control playback from the floating player without switching away from your current app.

The Services-menu path sends the selected words directly to LoudScript. The keyboard shortcut offers a faster system-wide workflow after Selected Text access is enabled in macOS Accessibility settings.

Where selected-text reading works

LoudScript is designed for standard Mac applications rather than one browser or document format. Use it with web articles, notes, messages, source code comments, email drafts, and selectable passages in document viewers.

Useful reading workflows

  • Proofread a draft by listening for missing words and repeated phrases.
  • Review study material while taking notes or resting your eyes.
  • Listen to a long article while continuing work in another window.
  • Check how names, numbers, and technical terms sound with different voices.

Selected Text access and privacy

The global shortcut uses macOS Accessibility access to retrieve highlighted text across applications. LoudScript explains this permission during onboarding, and you can review it later under Settings → Permissions.

Your speech text is not sent to product analytics. Apple voices and downloaded speech models synthesize speech on your Mac. If you explicitly enable optional AI formatting, the text being formatted can be sent to a third-party AI provider as described in the privacy policy.

What if the text cannot be selected?

Some apps render words inside images, videos, canvases, or locked interfaces. In that case, use LoudScript's screenshot OCR workflow to capture the relevant area and turn the visible words into speech.
