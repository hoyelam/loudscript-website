How to read a PDF aloud on Mac

Import the pages you need, listen with a local voice, and follow the current sentence in the original PDF and extracted text.

By Ho Ye Lam · Updated August 20, 2026

Read a PDF in four steps

  1. Open the PDF from LoudScript.
  2. Choose a page range. Each import can contain up to 50 consecutive pages.
  3. Select a voice and press Play. Apple voices and downloaded speech models synthesize on your Mac.
  4. Follow the highlighting. LoudScript marks the current sentence in both the document and extracted-text views.

What kind of PDFs work?

LoudScript extracts text from the pages you choose. It can also recognize words on image-based pages with Apple’s on-device Vision text recognition, so scanned reading material is supported when the print is clear enough.

Recognition accuracy depends on scan quality, language, layout, and handwriting. Password-protected or damaged files may need to be unlocked or repaired first.

PDF privacy and processing

PDF text extraction, OCR, and speech synthesis use local macOS frameworks or speech models downloaded to your Mac. LoudScript does not send raw PDF text to product analytics.

Optional AI formatting is separate. If you enable it, the text being formatted can be sent to the AI provider you configure. See the privacy policy for the exact boundary.

When LoudScript is a good PDF reader

  • You want to keep the original page visible while listening.
  • You need a focused page range instead of importing a whole book.
  • You want local speech and reading history in a native Mac app.
  • You need highlighting that follows playback across pages.

It is not an audiobook editor and does not currently position itself as a studio tool for exporting narrated audio files.

Try PDF text-to-speech on Mac

LoudScript is free for macOS 15.6 or later and supports Intel and Apple silicon Macs.
